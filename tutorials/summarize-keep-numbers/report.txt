Harbourline Ferry Cooperative: Annual Operations Report for 2025

This report is a made-up example written for a programming tutorial. The cooperative, its routes and every figure in it are invented.

Passengers and routes

Harbourline Ferry Cooperative ran 4 routes in 2025: the Pier Route, the Island Loop, the Northern Crossing and the Night Shuttle. Together they carried 1,284,500 passengers, up 6.3% from 1,208,300 in 2024. The Island Loop was the busiest route with 512,400 passengers. The Pier Route carried 398,200, the Northern Crossing carried 287,900 and the Night Shuttle carried 86,000. Weekend traffic made up 41% of all trips. The busiest single day was 19 July, with 9,870 passengers across all routes.

Fleet and crossings

The fleet stood at 7 vessels at the end of the year. Two vessels are 28 years old and five are under 12 years old. The fleet made 31,640 crossings in 2025, of which 30,916 ran on time. That is an on-time rate of 97.7%. A total of 724 crossings were late and 64 were cancelled. Of the 64 cancellations, 41 were caused by wind above 38 knots and 23 by engine faults. The average crossing took 22 minutes on the Pier Route and 35 minutes on the Northern Crossing.

Revenue and costs

Revenue was 18.6 million euros, up from 17.1 million in 2024. Ticket sales brought in 14.2 million euros, season passes 2.9 million euros and onboard shop sales 1.5 million euros. Operating costs were 17.3 million euros. Fuel cost 4.8 million euros, which is 27.7% of operating costs. Wages cost 7.9 million euros and maintenance cost 3.1 million euros. The operating surplus was 1.3 million euros, or 7.0% of revenue.

Fuel and emissions

The vessels burned 2.9 million litres of fuel, 8.4% less than the 3.17 million litres burned in 2024. The saving came from slower cruising speeds on the Northern Crossing, which cut that route's fuel use by 11%. Carbon dioxide emissions fell to 7,800 tonnes from 8,520 tonnes. The cooperative aims to reach 6,500 tonnes by 2028. One vessel was fitted with a hybrid engine in September at a cost of 2.2 million euros. It has run 1,430 hours on battery power since.

Safety and staff

The cooperative employed 186 people at year end, including 58 deck crew, 34 engineers and 22 ticket staff. Staff turnover was 9.5%, down from 12.1% the year before. There were 3 reportable injuries and no passenger injuries. Crews completed 4,200 hours of safety training, an average of 22.6 hours per employee. Drills were held 96 times, which is twice the monthly minimum of 4 per vessel.

Customer feedback

A survey of 3,150 passengers gave an overall satisfaction score of 4.3 out of 5. Punctuality scored 4.5 and cleanliness scored 4.1. The most common complaint was crowding on summer weekends, raised by 18% of respondents. The cooperative received 412 written complaints and 267 compliments. Staff answered 93% of complaints within 5 working days.

Plans for 2026

The board approved a budget of 21.0 million euros for 2026. It includes 6.4 million euros for a new vessel that will carry 320 passengers and enter service in March 2027. The cooperative plans to add 2 extra summer sailings on the Island Loop and to raise season pass prices by 3.5% from 1 April. Fuel use is forecast to fall by a further 5% as two more vessels get hybrid engines. Passenger numbers are forecast to reach 1.35 million.
